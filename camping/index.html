Hello!

I'm so happy we're doing this again. Last year we went out to western Massachusetts, and I'm hoping we can make it an annual autumn trip.

This year we're going further north, to Ethan Pond in Crawford Notch, up in the White Mountains. It's a small pond tucked into the woods below the Willey Range, with a campsite sitting right on it. We will pack in Friday evening, set up camp in the dusky dark (like last year), and spend both nights there.

Saturday we walk to Thoreau Falls — eighty feet of water sliding down open granite ledge, way out in the middle of nowhere. The walk there follows an old logging railroad grade through the woods, so it's almost completely flat. No summits this year, no scrambling, nobody getting dragged up anything. Just a genuinely beautiful walk to a genuinely beautiful waterfall, with plenty of time to wander and chill and explore.

Sunday we pack up and come home.

Seven of us: Andrew, Naomi, Iona, Nola, Adia, Aydin, Diego.

Other things we'll do

Journaling. That was so cool last year, we will do it again!

Music. Hopefully Naomi will bring her ukulele. If you play something else small, bring that too.

Solo night walks. If you want to, you can take a solo walk in the dark on Saturday night.

Stars. There's nothing around us for miles in any direction. Hope for clear skies!

S'mores. No campfires are allowed at this site, so we'll have to toast marshmallows over the camp stove. It's a little bit ridiculous, but still delicious.

Sunset over the pond. We'll be facing the right way.

We eat dinner in the car, so we don't have to set up camp and cook in the dark.

Then we hike in: 2.8 miles, about 1,600 feet up.

The first stretch is the hard part — a steep half mile while we climb out of the notch. Then we top out and the trail becomes an old road that rolls gently downhill to the pond. The last two miles are easy.

Most of this will be in the dark. We've done it before! We will all have headlamps and the hike is straightforward.

We set up, eat something warm, and go to bed.

Oct 3

Saturday

Thoreau Falls and Zealand Notch

The whole day is Thoreau Falls.

It's about 2.6 miles each way, almost entirely flat, on the old railroad grade heading northwest from the pond. At the end there's a short spur down to the falls: eighty feet off an open ledge.

About 5.4 miles round trip, with basically no climbing. There's mud in places past the pond, so wear the boots.

If everyone's feeling good we can push another mile into Zealand Notch, where the trail runs along the base of Whitewall Mountain underneath an enormous field of broken cliff. It's one of the most dramatic places in the Whites and it's flat walking to get there.

We turn around whenever we want. Nothing today is mandatory.

Pancakes and bacon for breakfast, pasta for dinner, s'mores after.

Good to know

  • Probably no cell service once we're in the notch.
  • We're camping at about 2,900 feet. Cold nights, but nothing extreme and nothing above treeline.
  • Food goes into a bear box at the site overnight. There are black bears in the Whites and this is completely routine.
  • There's a composting outhouse at the campsite.
  • No campfires are permitted here. Camp stove only.
  • Total walking: 2.8 miles in, about 5.4 on Saturday, 2.8 out. Nothing steep except that one half-mile stretch Friday.

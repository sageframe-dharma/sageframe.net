Andrew T. Marcus // Tyro Sageframe

Sageframe is a personal knowledge architecture — a system of systems where infrastructure, documentation, philosophy, and creative practice are unified under a single living taxonomy. Built in Obsidian, deployed on self-hosted infrastructure, and governed by its own cartographic discipline, Sageframe treats the act of organizing knowledge as a practice in itself: part engineering, part epistemology, part meditation. It draws on Zen concepts of impermanence and beginner's mind to formalize how one person learns, builds, maintains, and reflects — then makes that process legible, reproducible, and open. If Borges had a homelab and Wittgenstein wrote YAML, they might have built something like this. Learn more about Sageframe as a knowledge architecture.

Tyro Sageframe is the persona I work under — "tyro" literally means beginner — because when you throw yourself headfirst into programming, systems thinking, AI, and informatics from absolute zero, you might as well own the bit and name yourself after the journey.

The software and projects here are a byproduct of that practice: explorations from love or curiosity and tools built out of genuine need, released because others probably need them too. I'm interested in digital sovereignty — keeping your files yours, your tools local, your thinking your own. Professional work and consulting at atmarcus.net.

// method

The disciplines and frameworks the rest of the work is built with — how the making itself is done.

The Ho System 歩 live
A methodology for building real software with AI while keeping architectural authority and genuine understanding. The human directs and evaluates; the AI implements; a bounded ho (歩 — "step") is one planned work session, and a sequence of them carries a project from seed to production. Fully codified, published as skills, and walked in public through its own build records. This is "encode your craft into a system so a team can hit the bar without you in the room" — rendered as an actual system. Not vibe coding; the discipline that makes AI collaboration distinguishable from it.
hosystem.sageframe.net · open methodology
↗ the methodology — github/sageframe-no-kaji/ho-system ↗ published under *, Actually — the framework below
*, Actually live
A framework for making relational technical knowledge navigable — enter with a question, dial the depth you need, never read more than you need. A domain-blind engine weaves depth-layered notes into a fully static, terminal-aesthetic, keyboard-driven site; each subject is an instance that supplies only its content. SSH, Actually is the first instance; Ho System, Actually is the second. Domain-agnostic: any field where understanding means navigating relationships between concepts, not looking up isolated facts.
star-actually.sageframe.net
↗ SSH, Actually — the first instance ↗ research context — atmarcus.net/work/research
SSH, Actually released
The SSH guide that finally makes sense. Definition-first, zero-jargon treatment of SSH, key pairs, hardware security keys, and modern authentication — built around mental models, not commands. Companion commands reference included. 100% signal, 0% noise. The written source the *, Actually framework grew out of.

A research and engagement program investigating how people keep their identity, voice, and judgment in sustained AI collaboration — especially in thinking and writing. It develops its ideas through a published essay practice and builds instruments practitioners can actually use, now live at interference.sageframe.net. Essays on Substack →

Shodō 書道 live beta
Turns exported AI conversation history into a searchable, synthesizable knowledge base, running entirely on personal hardware. Surfaces the through-lines across months of practice — recurring questions, shifts in understanding — that stay invisible inside any single conversation. Semantic search is working; the synthesis layer and its own MCP server are in active build.
shodo.sageframe.net
Sutra 経 in development
A macOS-native writing environment where every piece is a git repository, but the writer never sees git — only Marks, Trails, Threads, Echoes, and Weaves. Makes the human writer's process visible and recoverable so that drift toward AI voice can be detected and resisted over time. Voice DNA integration planned as the first AI module post-MVP. Swift/SwiftUI.
source not yet public
Voice DNA live
A corpus-primary voice instrument: your writing in, an evidence-anchored voice profile out, compiled into a personal skill with two faces — a personalized audit ("where does this stop sounding like me?") and a voice framing ("make a session sound like me before it starts"). A mirror, not a judge. Self-DNA only. Learns a voice from a corpus or from a structured interview, so a writer with little writing still gets a complete profile.
interference.sageframe.net/voice-dna
Superposition live
A teaching system that walks a literate non-writer through producing a piece that is genuinely theirs — a talk, an essay, a toast, a letter. It finds what the person actually cares about, builds their voice from their reactions, drafts with the machine's guesses exposed, and refuses to ghostwrite. Uses Constructive Interference and Voice DNA underneath. Their material, their voice, their judgment.
interference.sageframe.net/superposition
Destructive Interference live
A diagnostic taxonomy of named structural failure classes in AI-influenced writing — the ways a draft drifts toward the machine's voice and away from the writer's. The naming is the tool: once a failure class has a name you can see it, and once you can see it you can resist it. In daily use, and the diagnostic layer the other instruments build on.
interference.sageframe.net/destructive-interference
Kiku 聴く live
Extracts classes of language behavior from AI conversation exports — a hybrid regex-plus-LLM tool that finds where the machine's language shifts over a long collaboration. Point it at a single conversation or a whole corpus; regex catches the obvious hits, semantic classification catches the register shifts no pattern could. Built to study how AI language changes across extended work.
interference.sageframe.net/kiku

// infrastructure & sovereignty

Tending your own machines and keeping your tools, data, and secrets yours. Several of these form the Kṣetra-Ops suite — no hidden causality, no automation without presence.

Hōzō 宝蔵 released
Wake-on-demand ZFS backup orchestrator. Runs entirely on your controller machine — uses syncoid to push ZFS snapshots to a remote backup box over SSH. No agent required on the remote; it only needs ZFS, SSH, and appropriate permissions.
open source · github/sageframe-no-kaji/hozo-sentinel
sageframe-mcp in production
A local MCP server that exposes a personal homelab as deterministic, typed tools — so any Claude session can ask which hosts exist, what runs where, and what's quietly broken, and get structured data back instead of re-deriving SSH commands from docs every time. The skills are the strategy; this is the move set. Built under the Ho System. Source private — it encodes a live infrastructure map.
private source · in production · sageframe-mcp.html →
Pālana पालन released beta
A native Mac app for tending homelab infrastructure — calm, keyboard-first, dual-pane. Every operation compiles to a plan before it runs: the exact commands, the transport, the auth path. Moves and copies run server-side over SSH — host to host, your machine never carries the bytes — and it speaks ZFS natively. Not a dashboard; a place to sit down and work. Swift/SwiftUI. First tool of the Kṣetra-Ops suite.
open source · GPL-3.0 · beta now available · github/sageframe-no-kaji/palana
↗ ho-process/ — built autonomously under the Ho Process; hands reserved for design & UI
Sharibako 舎利箱 in development
A reliquary for digital secrets. A native Mac app and CLI that hold your API keys and env vars in a calm, age-encrypted, git-backed vault — shaped to how you actually work, by project and by machine. Materialize secrets as .env files or inject them at runtime so nothing touches disk. Link a secret once, rotate it once, and every project updates. Good hygiene is the easy default. The CLI is complete and usable today; the Swift GUI follows. Swift + Python, GPL-3.0.
open source · GPL-3.0 · github/sageframe-no-kaji/sharibako
↗ the worked example anchoring the Ho System site
sage-zfs in development
A lightweight control plane and status workbench for a ZFS service-state and backup architecture built on sanoid and syncoid. Not a new backup engine — the missing map: for every dataset you care about, where's the source of truth, where should replicas live, how fresh are they, what policy applies, and what's stale or broken. Faithful to the real homelab topology first; generalizes to other sanoid/syncoid users later.
in development · first ho run on ChatGPT · expected within a week
Forteller in development
CLI tool for tending the relationship between a configuration vault and the machines it describes. A config file carries a pragma — a comment block declaring where it belongs and what should happen when it arrives. The file is both the artifact and the manifest. Push-only, vault-first, no agents, no YAML sprawl. Part of the Kṣetra-Ops suite.
open source · github/sageframe-no-kaji/forteller
